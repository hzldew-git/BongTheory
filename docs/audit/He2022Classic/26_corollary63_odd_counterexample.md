## Construction

Let `K` be a dyadic local field with absolute ramification index `e=2`.
Choose a valuation unit `u` with quadratic defect `d(u)=1`; the existence of
such a unit is supplied by the proved odd-layer unit-defect classification.
Consider the six BONG coefficients

```text
[1, -1, 1, -u, u*pi^2, -u].
```

Beli's constructive Lemma 4.3 realizes these coefficients as a good BONG
`a` of a rank-six lattice `L`.  Its order sequence is

```text
[0, 0, 0, 0, 2, 0].
```

The first two orders prove classic integrality.  The complete odd clause of
the formalized Theorem 1.1, including the signed prefix-defect and terminal
gap branches, proves that `L` is classic `3`-universal.

Now reorder the same six coefficients by swapping the last two entries:

```text
[1, -1, 1, -u, -u, u*pi^2].
```

The corresponding good BONG `d` has order sequence

```text
[0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 2].
```

This sequence is nondecreasing, so the realized lattice `D` is exactly the
integral orthogonal basis lattice generated by `d`.  Thus `D` is the diagonal
lattice with the same six coefficients as the displayed BONG of `L`, up to
the immaterial permutation of the final two diagonal entries.

Beli's Lemma 4.7 makes the complete order sequence of a good BONG invariant
under integral lattice isometry.  At the fifth entry the two sequences are
`2` and `0`, respectively.  Hence `L` and `D` are not integrally isometric.
This directly contradicts the conclusion
`M \cong \langle a_1,\ldots,a_{n+3}\rangle` of Corollary 6.3 for `n=3`.

The result does not claim that `L` has no other diagonal presentation.  That
stronger assertion is unnecessary: nonisometry to the diagonal lattice made
from the displayed BONG coefficients already falsifies the stated
conjunction.
